# Epic 1 Retrospective: Trustworthy Content Assessment Foundation

Status: done

## Epic Review

Epic 1 delivered the quality-assessment foundation for the rest of the pipeline. All 5 stories are complete and the full test suite passed at closeout (`36` tests). The epic established shared contracts first, then added pure assessment helpers, then current-state persistence for quality status.

### What Went Well

- The contract-first approach in Story 1.1 paid off. `app/content_models.py` became the stable base for later work instead of each story inventing its own record shape.
- Keeping `app/quality_assessment.py` pure made the later stories easier to test. Missing-content, junk-content, print-hostile, and low-confidence checks stayed deterministic and local.
- Review cycles were useful. They found real contract gaps in Story 1.1, Story 1.3, Story 1.4, and Story 1.5 before those issues spread into later epics.
- Story 1.5 forced the project to define a current-state persistence pattern instead of drifting into event-log behavior.

### What Was Hard

- Nested validation rules were easy to underspecify. We hit this in Story 1.1 for quality-signal payloads and again in Story 1.5 for nested `content_type` matching.
- Threshold-based detection needs conservative defaults. Story 1.3 briefly over-flagged ordinary bracketed chord sheets before the heuristic was corrected.
- Missing-file and malformed-file behavior needed explicit decisions. The persistence story had to distinguish empty state from broken state instead of treating both the same.

### Key Lessons

- Exact identity matters. `artist`, `title`, `song_key`, `content_type`, and `content_hash` all need to stay aligned or the state becomes ambiguous.
- Loaders should validate recoverably, not silently normalize away problems. That is the right pattern for local review files.
- Signals should stay explainable and deterministic. That kept the tests stable and made review findings actionable.
